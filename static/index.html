Architecture

Graph attention over a growing complex.

Each chain becomes a 655-dimensional node (640d ESM-2 language model + 5d pLDDT + 1d SASA + 9d monomer geometry from AlphaFold). Chain pairs are connected by 5-dimensional edges encoding structural and functional compatibility. A 3-layer GAT reads the partial complex and ranks every candidate next subunit.

655d nodes · 5d edges · ~1M params
Step 1 — Representation

Each subunit becomes a node in a graph.

PRISM encodes every protein chain using ESM-2 (640d language model embeddings) enriched with per-residue pLDDT confidence, surface accessibility (SASA), and 9 monomer-geometry features derived from AlphaFold structures (radius of gyration, sphericity, principal axis ratios). The result: a 655-dimensional feature vector per chain.

640d ESM-2 + 5d pLDDT + 1d SASA + 9d geom
Step 2 — Edge features

Pairwise interactions define the edges.

For each chain pair the model computes buried surface area (BSA), STRING PPI confidence, GO-term similarity, ESM cosine similarity, and Voronoi-inspired Cα contact density (number of CA-CA contacts within 8Å, normalised by chain sizes). These five edge features encode interface affinity between chain pairs.

5d edge features per pair · +VoroIF contact density
Step 3 — Assembly path

A GNN scores each possible next docking step.

At each assembly step, a 3-layer Graph Attention Network reads the current partial complex and scores all candidate chains. The highest-scored chain docks next. PRISM repeats this greedy process until the complex is fully assembled.

3 GAT layers · 4 heads · 128d hidden
Step 4 — Evaluation

Validated against experimentally determined assembly orders.

PRISM achieves τ = 1.00 on the Marsh 2013 benchmark (10 complexes, pulse-chase mass spectrometry ground truth), τ = 0.98 on a held-out experimental set, and τ = 0.92 on the independent Path-LZerD benchmark (21 complexes, never seen during training). Backed by recent AlphaFold interpretability work showing that complex assembly is governed by monomer-level structural geometry — precisely what PRISM's node features encode.

0.98 held-out · 1.00 Marsh 2013 · 0.92 Path-LZerD · 96% top-1

Protein Assembly Viewer

GNN-guided subunit ordering · PRISM v4 · 15K complex corpus

A tool for visualising how a protein complex assembles one subunit at a time. The model greedily picks the next best subunit to join the growing structure, based on four scoring signals. Step through manually, play automatically, or adjust the temperature to explore stochastic orderings.

The four scoring terms

SASA (40%) Surface-Accessible Surface Area proxy. Approximated by sequence length relative to total complex mass. Larger subunits nucleate or join earlier. ESM (35%) Sequence embedding similarity. Mean cosine similarity between the candidate's ESM-2 150M embedding and already-assembled subunits. Reflects evolutionary co-adaptation. GO (15%) Gene Ontology coherence. Jaccard similarity between the candidate's GO molecular-function terms and those of assembled subunits. Predicted terms (✦) augment experimental annotations. PPI (10%) Protein–protein interaction confidence. Fetched from STRING (score 0–1). Falls back to 0.5 if unavailable.

Metrics

Kendall's τ Rank correlation between predicted and ground-truth assembly order. τ=1 is perfect; τ=−1 is reversed. Ring complexes use max over all 2N cyclic rotations. τ sparkline Partial Kendall τ at each step — shows how early the model locks in the correct ordering. Radar chart 4-axis score profile (SASA/ESM/PPI/GO) for winner (indigo) vs runner-up (red dashed). Confidence bar Softmax probability of the chosen candidate. Delta = margin over runner-up.

Research roadmap

Equivariant GNN Replace the flat 646d ESM mean-pool with rotation-equivariant message passing (EGNN / SE(3)-Transformer) over chain centroids — should improve accuracy on large heteromers where spatial geometry matters. AlphaFold-Multimer (Tier 3) Unlock 7,738 sequence-only complexes by predicting structures with AF-Multimer, then re-scoring with the same BSA pipeline. Adds ~50% more training data. Hierarchical assembly Domain-decompose >12-subunit complexes (NPC, ribosome, proteasome) into modules, predict module order, then intra-module order — avoids the O(N!) blow-up at large N. Symmetry-aware loss Generalise Marsh ring augmentation to arbitrary cyclic/dihedral symmetry: fold the known symmetry of homomers into the label space so the model isn't penalised for equivalent orderings. Curriculum learning Train Tier 1 → Tier 2 → Tier 3 with increasing N_subunits per epoch. Lets the model learn simple dimers / trimers before tackling large rings. Ensembles + calibration Ensemble 3 seeds, average logits; extend single-temperature Platt scaling to per-N_subunits temperatures. Reduces variance on small complexes without hurting large ones.
ProtFunc GO-term predictions (✦) are fetched from the ProtFunc server. If unavailable, PRISM falls back to UniProt experimental annotations only.
